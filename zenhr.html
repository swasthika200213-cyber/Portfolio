Case study

ZenHR

An HR management dashboard designed from scratch, in light and dark themes.

Role

UI/UX Designer

Tools

Figma, Photoshop

Type

Course project

A concept dashboard I designed from scratch during my course. It is not affiliated with any company.

01

Problem & solution

ZenHR is designed to streamline the daily operations of HR professionals. It provides a centralized platform to manage employee data, monitor attendance and performance, and handle payroll.

The problem

HR teams manage employee data, attendance, leave, payroll and performance across scattered tools and spreadsheets. That makes it slow and error-prone to get a clear, up-to-date picture of the organization, and routine tasks mean jumping between disconnected systems, costing HR staff time they need for higher-value work.

The solution

A centralized HR dashboard that brings attendance, payroll, performance and employee data into one structured interface. Key metrics appear immediately on login, and persistent navigation keeps every workflow one click away, so HR staff act on information instead of hunting for it.

02

Information architecture

The dashboard is organized around the core HR workflows that need daily attention. Each lives as its own section in a persistent left-hand navigation, so users can move between workflows without losing track of where they are.

MainDashboard, Employee, Attendance
PeoplePerformance, Payroll, Documentation
ProfileSettings, Help

The top-level dashboard leads with the metrics HR checks most often (total employees, present, absent and working from home) as quick-glance stat cards before any detailed table. This was deliberate: the busiest HR days start with a fast status check before deeper work, so the highest-frequency information is visible without scrolling or clicking in.

03

Wireframes

Early wireframes focused on getting the information hierarchy right before any visual styling: testing how much data could sit on the dashboard view without becoming overwhelming, and confirming that the sidebar could grow without feeling cluttered.

04

Design decisions

Answer the first question first

Four summary cards sit at the top, each with the change against last month and a small trend line, so the day’s picture is clear before anything is read.

One filter pattern everywhere

Employment type and period filters sit above the table on both the dashboard and payroll, with “Add New Employee” in the same place. The controls are learned once.

Status you can’t miss

Payroll status combines an icon, a word and a colour: a tick for success, an hourglass for pending, a cross for failed. It never relies on colour alone.

Two themes, one structure

The layout is identical in light and dark. Light uses a deep maroon accent, and dark uses a brighter red on charcoal so the accent stays visible on each background.

Chosen for its clean, modern look and high readability, and because its balanced structure suits the dark UI. Regular, Medium, Semibold, Bold.
